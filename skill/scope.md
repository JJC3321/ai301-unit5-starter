## Where candidates come from

Any public repository. The sandbox is behind you; the field is now
all of GitHub, and your fit profile below is what keeps that
searchable. Start from repos in languages and tools you named there,
and let the profile steer where you search, not just how results are
ranked.

**Claims count again.** The Path Review house rule does not travel.
Out here a claim comment or an assignee is a stranger who got there
first, and you have no channel to coordinate with them. Read claim
signals exactly as the rubric's checks state them; do not carry the
classroom habit of claiming over someone.

**Claim upstream the moment you commit.** The rule cuts both ways:
the instant you decide to take an issue, say so on the issue itself
(a short comment: what you plan to do and roughly when). You are now
the claim signal someone else's skill will read. Deciding privately
and claiming later is how two strangers do the same work twice.

## Your fit profile

For my real-world contributions, I prioritize C++ desktop applications, libraries, and systems/tooling projects (such as Qt/C++ GUI plugins, scientific tools, and data-structure-heavy libraries), since I write C++ regularly for systems and DSA work and want hands-on experience shipping C++ changes in production open-source codebases. I am also comfortable in Python (FastAPI backends, data ingestion, CLI and AI developer tooling) and TypeScript/React as a secondary track when C++ candidates are thin. I strongly prefer bounded bug fixes and small behavioral or accessibility improvements that come with clear acceptance criteria, reproducible steps, or an existing in-repo pattern to follow. Rank lower or avoid: vague design debates, umbrella tracking issues, documentation-only typo or pure CSS/copy polish with no behavior change, and issues that require inventing product policy or redesigning core internals.
